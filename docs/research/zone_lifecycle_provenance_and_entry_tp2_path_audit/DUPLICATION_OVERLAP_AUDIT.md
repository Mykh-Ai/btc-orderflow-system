# Duplication and overlap audit

The two source branches have different meanings. A significant zone is a 30-day accumulation-window aggregate (`market_monitor/accumulation_zones.py:24-59`; `market_monitor/significant_market_zones.py:79-133,136-219`); a registry zone is a merge of structure-derived liquidity levels within the last 1440 minutes in Snapshot V2 (`market_monitor/structure.py:39-65`; `market_monitor/liquidity_zones.py:128-150,153-250`; `docs/research/open_trade_outcome_predictor_v1/snapshot_v2.py:149-167`). `source_level_ids` and `source_timeframes` trace registry constituents **within one rebuild**; significant `evidence_json` samples up to 20 `accumulation_zone_*` IDs, with no foreign key to structure `level_*` IDs (`significant_market_zones.py:181-188`). Thus overlap can signal related price memory, but cannot prove shared origin or independent barriers.

## Matching counterexamples and limit

- **A, one significant → many registry:** all 18 selected significant zones with a match had 2–23 geometric overlap candidates (`ZONE_IDENTITY_MATRIX.csv`). The matching function does not filter side or status (`snapshot_v2.py:219-228`).
- **B, one registry → many significant:** Jul 18 `zone_000003`, `000004`, and `000005` each overlap both significant `000001` and `000002` on the entry→TP2 interval; Aug 31 `zone_000006` overlaps three significant intervals (`ENTRY_TP2_PATH_AUDIT.csv`).
- **C, nearest geometric vs source:** The May 22 short's chosen `zone_000004` is BUY_SIDE although the path's opposing side is SELL_SIDE. Its `level_*` source IDs have no recorded bridge to the aggregate's `accumulation_zone_*` sources. We can prove an unsupported mapping and side mismatch, **not** prove those price histories have no economic relationship.
- **D, lifecycle disagreement:** The May 22 chosen registry row is `CROSSED_UNCLASSIFIED`/`CHOPPED_THROUGH`, whereas the significant aggregate remains `SIGNIFICANT` and combines 105 source windows. These statuses describe different entities and have different definitions; their difference is not a logical contradiction. It shows why copying one row's lifecycle to the aggregate is semantically unsafe.

Registry merge lineage is local: same-side compatible rows coalesce, source-level IDs/timeframes union, and predecessor rows retain `MERGED → merged_into_zone_id` (`zone_registry.py:375-544`). This can identify duplicate references in a persisted run, but Snapshot invokes the registry with no prior state and rebuilds `level_*` IDs. Significant-zone suppression removes aggregates when overlap/min-width reaches 50% (`significant_market_zones.py:271-290`); it does not deduplicate registry-versus-significant views or all touching/partially overlapping significant intervals. The ordered path therefore cannot safely assume independence from the remaining rows.

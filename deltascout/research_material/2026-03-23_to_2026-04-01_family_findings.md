# DeltaScout Research State Handoff

## Purpose

This file preserves the current research-state conclusion after the first family-level decomposition of the short-side reject funnel.

It exists as context for future DeltaScout analysis.

It is **not**:
- a full batch summary
- an agent task
- a live-logic recommendation
- a setup-validation document

Its purpose is to keep future analysis aligned with the current evidence base and prevent regression into overly broad reject-pool thinking.

---

## Current research state

The reviewed material has moved beyond the earlier stage of:

- generic reject-funnel review
- dominant reject-reason counting
- sparse accepted-flow acknowledgment

The current research state is now:

- still reject-heavy
- still accepted-sparse
- but structurally rich enough to justify **family-level decomposition** of the short-side reject funnel

The strongest current research surface is still in rejects, not in accepted events.

Accepted events remain a **reference class**, not the boundary of thinking.

---

## Current accepted-flow status

Within the reviewed batch, accepted-flow remains too sparse to define a stable accepted reference class.

Current accepted-flow conclusion:

- accepted short cases are useful only as **reference examples**
- they do **not** currently define a validated edge source
- current accepted flow looks more like a **narrow survival path through the funnel** than a broad and robust selection of the strongest short-side structures
- accepted cases are not yet clearly stronger than nearby same-session reject alternatives
- current accepted-flow weakness looks more like weak structural separation than one single repeated `SL` mechanism

This means future analysis should not become PEAK-centric.

---

## Accepted-flow refinement after SL postmortem

Later accepted-SL postmortem work did not support one single common immediate failure mode across the accepted short `PEAK` `SL` cases.

- one accepted case looks closer to fast invalidation / continuation against entry
- the other looks closer to a narrower survival that initially behaved correctly and then failed later
- the stronger shared weakness is not one identical failure path, but weak structural separation from nearby same-session reject alternatives
- current accepted flow should still be treated as reference-class diagnostics, not as a validated edge source
- this accepted-flow reading strengthens the view that `PEAK` is one important research direction, but not the center of the whole research program

---
## Main research shift

The most important current conclusion is:

> the short-side reject funnel should no longer be treated as one undifferentiated reject population.

Current evidence supports working with at least two distinct short-side research families.

---

## Family A

### Operational definition

Family A consists of short-side rejects where:

- `price_vs_vwap_side=above`
- the visible rejection is often `vwap_side`
- nearby same-session short-side conflict rows may be included when they belong to the same transition cluster

### Current interpretation

Family A currently looks like an **earlier transition family**.

Working read:
- short-side pressure is beginning to form
- but structural placement remains conflicted
- the move has not yet fully crossed into structurally aligned short-side territory

### Current research role

Family A is currently the strongest **transition-timing lead**.

It is most useful for studying:

- when early short pressure starts becoming meaningful
- how same-session short attempts accumulate before structural confirmation
- where short pressure is still too early versus where it is becoming actionable research material

### Important caution

Family A is not currently a validated setup class.

It should not be used to justify mechanical loosening of `vwap_side`.

---

## Family B

### Operational definition

Family B consists of short-side rejects where:

- `price_vs_vwap_side=below`
- short-side placement is already structurally aligned
- the case still fails deeper in the funnel
- the visible rejection is often `3of3_fail`
- nearby deeper-failure short-side conflict rows may be included when supported by sequence context

### Current interpretation

Family B currently looks like a **later transition family**.

Working read:
- short-side structural placement has already aligned
- the candidate is more mature than Family A
- but the move still fails on deeper comparison, timing, or sequence constraints

### Current research role

Family B is currently the stronger **broad setup-discovery lane**.

It is most useful for studying:

- why already aligned short-side candidates still fail
- whether deeper failure is caused by timing, sequence conflict, exhaustion, trap behavior, or grammar mismatch
- which Family B subgroups may later justify future setup-class research

### Important caution

Family B is not yet a validated profitable setup family.

It is only the strongest current candidate zone for deeper research.

---

## Current working model

The best current working model is:

- **Family A** = earlier short-side transition with placement conflict
- **Family B** = later short-side transition with alignment already achieved but deeper failure still present

This model is strong enough to guide future analysis.

It is **not yet** strong enough to claim:
- that the two families are definitively separate setup classes
- that one should be promoted into live logic
- that current funnel rules should be loosened

---

## Strongest current research conclusion

The main research pressure is no longer simply:

- `vwap_side` blocks interesting short cases

It is now:

- some short-side candidates fail **before** structural placement completes
- other short-side candidates fail **after** structural placement has already aligned

This means the short-side funnel is suppressing candidates at **at least two different structural stages**.

This is the key handoff conclusion.

---

## What future analysis should preserve

Future analysis should preserve all of the following:

1. Accepted short cases remain **reference class only**.
2. Rejects remain the primary research surface.
3. Family A and Family B should be analyzed separately.
4. Do not collapse the short-side reject funnel back into one generic pool.
5. Do not treat bucket labels as validated setup classes.
6. Do not recommend mechanical filter loosening without stronger evidence.
7. Keep distinguishing:
   - early placement conflict
   - later deeper failure after alignment

## What is still unknown

The current state does **not** yet prove:

- that Family A and Family B are fully separate setup classes
- that Family B would outperform the accepted reference class in live trading
- that `vwap_side` is too strict
- that `3of3_fail` is wrong
- that current accepted flow is selecting the best short-side structures
- that the strongest rejected Family B cases would have produced better real trade outcomes

These remain open research questions.
